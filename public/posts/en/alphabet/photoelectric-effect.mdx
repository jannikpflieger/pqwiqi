---
title: "Photoelectric Effect"
subtitle: "Emission of electrons caused by suitable light"
author: "Melin Kayapınar"
date: "2026-08-14"
letter: "P"
---

If an object is irradiated with light of a suitable wavelength, the photons can be absorbed by electrons in the atomic shells. This can cause electrons to be emitted from the object, a phenomenon known as the photoelectric effect.

This property of light supports the existence of photons, because the occurrence of the effect depends on the wavelength, and therefore the energy, of the photons. The intensity of the light, meaning the number of photons, is not the decisive factor.
