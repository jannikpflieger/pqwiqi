If someone says that it is 25 °C outside, we know what to expect. But what precisely does temperature measure?

The outside air, like all other objects such as metal or water, is made up of tiny particles. Each of these particles contains a certain amount of kinetic energy. Thus, the particles vibrate and move in all directions on a microscopic scale, even if the larger system appears calm.

What we call temperature is the measurement of the average kinetic energy of all particles in a system.
