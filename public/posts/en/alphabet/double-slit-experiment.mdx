title: "Double-Slit Experiment"
subtitle: "A key experiment showing wave-like behaviour in quantum particles"
author: "Melin Kayapınar"
date: "2026-08-14"
letter: "D"
---

If you throw balls at a barrier with two slits, they can simply pass through either slit. Behind the barrier, the balls cluster around the slits. This makes sense: the balls do not change direction and simply fly straight through.

With electrons or other quantum particles, however, this is not observed. If they encounter a double slit, they create an interference pattern on the other side. This usually occurs when waves overlap, either amplifying or cancelling each other. Thus, electrons must also have wave properties.

If you then try to measure which slit the electron passed through, it appears to lose its wave properties and behaves like a ball that has simply flown straight through one slit.
