# Quantum Hyperpolarisation

When normal scans, such as NMR or MRI, are used at room temperature, nuclei that behave like tiny spinning magnets will point in random directions, causing scans to detect roughly 1 out of every 100,000 spins. This is where Quantum Hyperpolarization comes into play. This forces the nucleus's spin to align in the desired direction, amplifying a weak, otherwise undetectable signal.

## Case in which Quantum Hyperpolarisation is used

Normal MRI machines work by detecting signals from water molecules in the body, which is why MRI is fantastic for imaging soft tissue like the brain or muscles. But lungs are mostly empty air space, with very little water, so on a regular MRI, lungs look like a dark, useless blur.

Hence, Cases such as early lung disease diagnosis are challenging to assess with proton-based lung MRI since they're mostly air with few water protons. Therefore, techniques such as hyperpolarized gas MRI use the spin-1/2 isotope $^3He$. 

>When human hyperpolarized gas MRI studies are carried out, the subject typically inhales the gas from a small plastic bag while positioned inside the bore, and the measurement starts immediately at breath-hold. Helium remains almost entirely within the air spaces after inhalation and does not cause any serious side effects after a single breath. _Source:Kern AL, Vogel-Claussen J. Hyperpolarized gas MRI in pulmonology. Br J Radiol. 2018 Apr;91(1084):20170647. doi: 10.1259/bjr.20170647. Epub 2018 Jan 22. PMID: 29271239; PMCID: PMC5965996._

Scientists are also using the hyperpolarization technique in quantum sensing. Physicists used a single defect in a diamond crystal called a nitrogen-vacancy (NV) center as an incredibly precise quantum probe to hyperpolarize nearby molecules, achieving this at room temperature without requiring extreme cold or bulky equipment. This makes the adaptation of hyperpolarisation favorable because it is cheaper.

In conclusion, the widespread application of hyperpolarisation will make it more likely that researchers will use it in the future.
