# Quantum Superabsorption

## What is quantum superabsorption?

Imagine again a group of atoms trying to absorb light. Normally, each atom absorbs photons independently. So, increasing the number of atoms, increases the absorption rate of light. 

However, quantum physics allows something more interesting.

When atoms are prepared in a special collective quantum state, they no longer behave as
separate particles. Instead, they act like a single “giant atom". The interaction with light becomes cooperative, and the absorption increases faster compared to linear absorption rate of independent atom with respect to the number of atoms.
- **Independent absorption:** $R \propto N$
- **Superabsorption:** $R \propto N^2$

where **R** is rate of absorption and **N** is number of atoms.

The key idea behind superabsorption is **quantum coherence**. In classical physics, particles
act independently. But in quantum mechanics, particles can share a collective wavefunction.
This creates correlations between them, even if they are physically separate.
A useful way to picture this is to consider independent atoms as many small antennas each collecting light on its own and collective quantum system as one large antenna that captures light cooperatively. Because of this collective behavior, a quantum system can absorb light far more efficiently than the sum of its individual parts, leading to the phenomenon known as quantum superabsorption

## Why do we want it?
The main advantage is enhanced energy transfer. Instead of improving devices by simply
adding more material, superabsorption allows us to boost performance and Absorb energy
faster

![Single-photon superabsorption in solid-state emitters as the time reversal of single-photon superradiance](/posts/assets/entries/qsuperabsorption/absorb_size.png)
_Image Source:[Boehme, S.C., Nguyen, T.P.T., Zhu, C. et al. Single-photon superabsorption in CsPbBr3 perovskite quantum dots. Nat. Photon. 19, 864–870 (2025)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41566-025-01684-3?fromPaywallRec=false)

Although still experimental stage, quantum superabsorption could have important applications:
1. More efficient solar energy -
In conventional solar cells, photons are absorbed independently by electrons in a material.
Superabsorption could enable faster and more efficient energy capture, leading to 
development of next-generation photovoltaic materials with improved performance.
2. Faster quantum batteries -
Quantum batteries store energy in quantum states instead of chemical bonds.
With superabsorption, charging time can decrease significantly with charging rate increasing rapidly than the
number of battery components.
3. Ultra-sensitive light detection -
For very weak signals (like in astronomy or quantum communication) detecting a single
photon can be difficult. Superabsorption will amplify interaction with light which could lead to
better telescopes, improved quantum communication systems, and more precise sensing and measurement devices.
