# Quantum Tech in Healthcare

When you hear the word **quantum** you might think of science-fiction movies or complicated physics 
equations scribbled across a chalkboard. 
However, the principles of quantum mechanics - the rules that governs the behaviour of atoms and 
subatomic particles are 
rapidly becoming powerful tools in modern medicine.

This article explores how the unusual phenomena in quantum world are being harnessed to enable 
early disease diagonsis, improve drugs discovery, 
and understand the human body in entirely new ways.

## What is "Quantum" Anyway?

To understand quantum medicine, we first need a basic understanding of **quantum physics**. 
In our everyday world, things follow predictable, 
"classical" rules. If you throw a ball, you know exactly where it will land.

However, when you zoom in to the size of atoms, those rules breakdown. Particles can be in 
multiple states at once - a phenomenon known as superposition, or 
become invisibly connected over distances - a phenomenon called entanglement.

For decades, these strange rules were just theories or laboratory experiments. Today, scientists 
are harnessing these rules to build a new technology. 
When quantum technologies are applied to healthcare and medicine, they are often referred to as 
**quantum medical technologies**.

##  Three ways Quantum Physics is Changing Medicine

### 1. Ultra-Precise Medical Imaging

You might be surprised to learn that hospitals already use a "quantum" device every day: 
the **MRI (Magnetic Resonance Imaging)** machine.

![image.png]((/posts/assets/entries/qmedicine/mri_machine.png))
_Image Sources:[Riverwood Healthcare Center](https://riverwoodhealthcare.org/riverwood-healthcare-center-introduces-new-mri-machine/)

MRIs work by interacting with the quantum "spin" of the hydrogen atoms inside your body to 
create detailed pictures of your organs. 
However, the next generation of quantum imaging goes much further. Scientists are developing 
**quantum sensors** that are incredibly 
sensitive to magnetic and electrical fields.

- **Heart and Brain Mapping:** Because our brains and hearts run on tiny electrical impulses, 
highly sensitive quantum sensors 
(like lab-grown diamonds with specific atomic flaws) can map this activity with unprecedented detail.
- **Early Cancer Detection:** These sensors are becoming so sensitive they might soon be able to 
detect the tiny magnetic signatures 
of cancer cells long before a tumor is visible on a traditional scan.

### 2. Quantum Computing for Drug Discovery

Creating a new medicine is incredibly difficult. It requires finding a chemical molecule that 
fits perfectly into a target in the body, like a key into a lock.

Currently, scientists use supercomputers to simulate these molecular "keys." But molecules are 
quantum systems, and classical computers struggle to calculate the incredibly complex quantum 
interactions of large proteins.

**Quantum computers**, on the other hand, operate using the exact same rules as the molecules themselves.

![image.png](/posts/assets/entries/qmedicine/quantum_comp.png)
_Image Sources:[Jeremy Hsu, Measuring Progress in the 'Noisy' Era of Quantum Computing ](https://spectrum.ieee.org/measuring-progress-in-the-noisy-era-of-quantum-computing)
- They can simulate chemical reactions with perfect accuracy.
- They can process millions of molecular combinations in a fraction of the time.
- This means we could soon design custom drugs for complex diseases like Alzheimer's or 
tailor-make treatments for an individual's specific DNA.

### 3. Quantum Biology

![image.png](/posts/assets/entries/qmedicine/quantum_bio.png)
_Image Sources:[Dreamstime.com](https://www.dreamstime.com/scientist-holding-glass-vial-blue-dna-molecule-d-rendering-closeup-gloved-hands-open-containing-strands-medical-cap-image328658658)


This is perhaps the most cutting-edge and mysterious area of all. **Quantum biology** asks the question: 

**Does nature already use quantum physics to keep us alive?**

Researchers are discovering that certain biological processes might actually rely on quantum mechanics. 
For example:

- **Enzymes:** The proteins that speed up chemical reactions in our bodies might use a quantum 
trick called "tunneling" to move particles through solid barriers.
- **DNA Mutations:** Some scientists suspect that quantum fluctuations could play a role in how DNA 
mutates, which is fundamental to both evolution and diseases like cancer.

Understanding these natural quantum tricks could lead to entirely new ways of treating illnesses at 
their most fundamental source.

## The Future of Quantum Healthcare

It is important to note that while quantum sensors are entering clinical trials today, large-scale 
quantum computers capable of designing new drugs are still some years away.

However, the transition has already begun. As these technologies mature, medicine will likely shift 
from treating diseases after they cause damage, to predicting and preventing them at the atomic level.
