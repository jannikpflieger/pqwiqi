# Quantum Hyperpolarisation
Every atom generates a small magnetic field through its spin. These magnetic fields are aligned, and if multiple fields are aligned in the same direction, they amplify each other. Simultaneously, if they are more randomly aligned, they cancel each other out.

## Quantum Hyperpolarisation in Cancer Treatment

When looking for cancer cells in the body, one way is to observe the magnetic fields of molecules in the body with an MRI. While this method does not require radioactive materials, it can only measure magnetic fields if they are strong enough. Therefore, the magnetic fields have to be amplified to a measurable size, which is (with current technology) either very expensive, unreliable, or slow.
This is where Quantum Hyperpolarisation comes into play. By using a special magnetic state of hydrogen atoms, it is possible to align all the magnetic fields so that they amplify one another, making them much easier to detect and observe.
