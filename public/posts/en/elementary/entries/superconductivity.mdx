## Introduction

Imagine you are riding a bicycle on a road full of bumps and stones. As you move forward, you slow down, lose energy, and have to keep pedalling. This is similar to how electricity behaves in normal materials like the wires in your home: as it flows, it keeps “bumping” into things inside the material and loses energy as heat.

Now imagine a perfectly smooth, magical road where you can ride your bike forever without slowing down, even if you stop pedalling. That is what happens in a superconductor.

Superconductivity is a special state where electricity can flow with no resistance at all, meaning no energy is lost.

## How Electricity Works in Everyday Materials

In normal materials, electricity is carried by tiny particles called electrons. As they move, they bump into atoms inside the material. These bumps slow them down and turn some of the energy into heat. That’s why:

* Wires and electronic devices can get warm
* Energy is lost when electricity travels long distances

## What Happens in a Superconductor

In a superconductor, something very unusual happens. When the material is cooled to extremely low temperatures, the electrons can move through the material in a coordinated way that avoids these collisions.

Superconductors also behave in surprising ways with magnets. They can push magnetic fields away, which can make magnets float above them!

## Experiments and Discovery

Superconductivity was discovered in 1911 by Heike Kamerlingh Onnes. He was studying a metal called mercury and cooled it to very low temperatures, much colder than anything we experience in daily life. Suddenly, its electrical resistance dropped to zero.

Since then, scientists have found many materials that can become superconductors, although most need to be kept extremely cold.

## Why Is Superconductivity Important?

Superconductors are already useful in real life. One important example is the MRI scanners used in hospitals. MRI (Magnetic Resonance Imaging) machines take detailed pictures of the inside of the body. To do this, they need very strong and stable magnets, and superconductors help create these magnets without wasting energy.

Right now, most superconductors only work when they are extremely cold, which makes them difficult and expensive to use. Scientists are trying to find materials that work at warmer temperatures. If they succeed, we could have power lines that don’t waste energy and many new amazing technologies.
