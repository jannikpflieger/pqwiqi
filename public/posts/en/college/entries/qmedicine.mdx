# Quantum Tech in Healthcare

When we hear the word **quantum**, we often think of ideas like superposition, entanglement, or uncertainty. These concepts can seem far removed from everyday life, let alone medicine. But quantum mechanics is already behind some technologies used in healthcare, and researchers are now exploring how more advanced quantum technologies could be used in medicine.

From highly sensitive sensors to molecular simulations, quantum technology could give us new ways to **measure, understand, and treat biological systems**.

## What Makes a Technology "Quantum"?

At the scale of atoms and subatomic particles, nature follows the rules of **quantum mechanics**. These rules allow physical systems to behave in ways that are very different from what we see in everyday life.

For example, a quantum state can exist as a **superposition** of different states:

$$
|\psi\rangle = \alpha|0\rangle + \beta|1\rangle,
$$

where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ describe the probabilities of obtaining each state when the system is measured.

Quantum technologies are built by controlling such quantum properties and using them for a specific purpose. In healthcare, this could mean making measurements more sensitive or simulating molecular systems more efficiently.

## Three Ways Quantum Physics Could Change Medicine

### 1. Quantum Sensing and Medical Imaging

One familiar medical technology that relies on quantum physics is **MRI (Magnetic Resonance Imaging)**.

![image.png](/posts/assets/entries/qmedicine/mri_machine.png)

*Image Source: [Riverwood Healthcare Center](https://riverwoodhealthcare.org/riverwood-healthcare-center-introduces-new-mri-machine/)*

MRI makes use of the **spin** of atomic nuclei, particularly hydrogen nuclei, which are abundant in the human body. When these nuclei are placed in a strong magnetic field, they respond to radio-frequency signals. The resulting signals can then be used to build detailed images of the body's internal structures.

Researchers are now developing even more sensitive **quantum sensors**. One example is the **nitrogen-vacancy (NV) center in diamond**—a tiny defect in the diamond lattice with useful quantum properties.

NV centers are highly sensitive to magnetic fields, which makes them interesting for studying the tiny magnetic signals produced by biological activity.

They could potentially be used to study:

* **Brain activity**, by detecting weak magnetic fields produced by neurons;
* **Heart activity**, by measuring magnetic signals associated with cardiac activity;
* **Cellular processes**, where extremely small magnetic signals may contain useful information.

These applications are still being researched, but the sensitivity of quantum sensors makes them promising tools for future biomedical measurements.

### 2. Quantum Computing for Drug Discovery

Designing a new drug involves understanding how molecules interact with proteins and other biological targets. This is difficult because molecules themselves are quantum systems.

In principle, their behavior is described by the Schrödinger equation:

$$
\hat{H}|\psi\rangle = E|\psi\rangle.
$$

As molecules become more complex, calculating their quantum states becomes increasingly demanding for classical computers.

This is where **quantum computing** could eventually help. Quantum computers use qubits and quantum operations to represent and manipulate quantum states, potentially making them useful for certain molecular simulations.

![image.png](/posts/assets/entries/qmedicine/quantum_comp.png)

*Image Source: [Jeremy Hsu, "Measuring Progress in the 'Noisy' Era of Quantum Computing"](https://spectrum.ieee.org/measuring-progress-in-the-noisy-era-of-quantum-computing)*

Researchers are exploring quantum computing for problems such as:

* calculating molecular electronic structure;
* studying chemical reactions;
* understanding drug-target interactions;
* searching for promising drug candidates.

There is still a long way to go. Current quantum computers are affected by **noise, decoherence, and gate errors**, so classical computers remain essential for most practical drug-discovery tasks. The hope is that future, more reliable quantum computers will become useful for problems where classical simulations become difficult.

### 3. Quantum Biology

Perhaps the most interesting question is whether quantum mechanics plays an active role in biology itself. This is the focus of **quantum biology**.

Biological systems are warm, complex, and constantly interacting with their surroundings, which makes maintaining quantum coherence difficult. Even so, researchers have found several biological processes where quantum effects may be relevant.

![image.png](/posts/assets/entries/qmedicine/quantum_bio.png)

*Image Source: [Dreamstime](https://www.dreamstime.com/scientist-holding-glass-vial-blue-dna-molecule-d-rendering-closeup-gloved-hands-open-containing-strands-medical-cap-image328658658)*

* **Enzymes:** Quantum tunneling of electrons or protons may contribute to certain enzymatic reactions.
* **Photosynthesis:** Researchers are studying whether quantum effects influence how energy moves through photosynthetic systems.
* **DNA:** Proton tunneling has been proposed as one possible factor in certain forms of DNA mispairing and mutation.

These ideas are still being investigated, and they do not mean that the human body is simply a "quantum computer." Instead, quantum biology looks for specific situations where quantum effects can make a measurable difference to biological processes.

## The Future of Quantum Healthcare

Quantum technology is still an emerging field in healthcare, but its potential is becoming increasingly interesting.

**Quantum sensors** could help scientists measure biological signals that are currently difficult to detect. **Quantum computers** could eventually provide new ways to study molecules and chemical reactions, while **quantum biology** may help us understand whether nature itself makes use of quantum effects in important biological processes.

The aim is not to replace conventional medicine with "quantum medicine." Instead, quantum technologies could give researchers and doctors **new ways to measure, simulate, and understand the human body**.

What started as an attempt to understand the strange behavior of matter at the smallest scales is now opening new possibilities for some of the biggest challenges in healthcare.
